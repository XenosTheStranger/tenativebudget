Where does the money come from?

From two places: our community, and sources outside it. Some money comes from the people who live, work, own property, or use services here. Other money comes from visitors and outside partners. Together, these sources help pay for the services our community depends on.

Funding generated here

Money from Our Community

Residents, property owners, businesses, and people using County services provide this money. It is collected here in Walton County and helps support local services.

FY 2027 from our community$218.0 millionIncludes the resident share of sales taxes, property taxes, permits, service charges, fines, interest, and other locally generated operating income.
Property Taxes$161.1MHome, land, and business property owners

What it is: A tax based on taxable property value.

Who pays: Owners of taxable homes, land, and businesses.

Why the County receives it: It provides steady support for services used across the county.

For example: It helps fund public safety, roads, parks, libraries, and other daily services.

Permits & Licenses$2.0MPeople and businesses requesting approval

What it is: A charge for reviewing an application or issuing permission.

Who pays: Builders, property owners, contractors, and businesses applying for permits or licenses.

Why the County receives it: The fee helps cover the work needed to review plans and applications.

For example: Building-permit fees help support plan review and inspections.

Service Charges$15.2MPeople who use certain County services

What it is: A payment tied to a specific service.

Who pays: The person or organization using that service.

Why the County receives it: The charge helps pay part of the cost of providing the service.

For example: Ambulance, recreation, golf, or waste-service charges help support those services.

Special Assessments$1.4MProperties receiving a special benefit

What it is: A charge connected to a service or improvement that benefits certain properties.

Who pays: The owners of the properties receiving that benefit.

Why the County receives it: It connects the cost to the properties that benefit most directly.

For example: An assessment may help pay for a neighborhood road, lighting, or fire service.

Local Taxes$23.5MResident sales taxes and other local taxes

What it is: The resident-paid share of sales taxes plus other smaller taxes collected locally.

Who pays: Residents making taxable purchases and people or businesses responsible for other local taxes.

Why the County receives it: State and local rules return or direct a portion of these collections to County government.

For example: Everyday purchases and other taxable activity help support eligible County services.

Other Local Income$14.9MInterest, rentals, fines, and local payments

What it is: Money earned or received locally that does not fit the larger sources above, including fines and court-related collections.

Who pays: It may come from renters, buyers of County property, local agreements, financial institutions, or people responsible for fines.

Why the County receives it: It makes use of County assets and money already on hand and includes collections directed to the County by law.

For example: Interest earned before budgeted money is spent and court-related fines can help support County needs.

Funding brought into Walton County

Money from Outside Our Community

Visitors, travelers, the State of Florida, and the federal government also contribute. This money brings outside dollars into Walton County and helps keep local residents from carrying the entire cost alone.

FY 2027 from outside our community$110.4 millionIncludes the visitor share of sales taxes, Tourist Development Taxes, and state, federal, and other government revenue.
Tourist Development Tax$59.0MGuests staying in short-term lodging

What it is: A tax on short-term lodging stays.

Who pays: Guests at hotels, condos, and vacation rentals.

Why the County receives it: Tourism brings added activity and service needs to Walton County.

For example: The money supports uses allowed by tourist-tax law, such as beach-related work and tourism services.

State-Shared Money$8.0MTaxes collected and returned by Florida

What it is: Part of certain taxes collected by the State of Florida and shared with local governments.

Who pays: People and businesses paying those taxes across Florida.

Why the County receives it: State law directs a share back to counties.

For example: Walton County can use eligible shared money to help support local public services.

Federal Grants$3.1MFunding awarded by the federal government

What it is: Money provided for an approved program or project.

Who pays: It comes from federal tax collections.

Why the County receives it: Walton County qualifies or applies for funding tied to a public need.

For example: A federal grant may support housing, emergency response, transportation, or recovery work.

State Grants$69,588Funding awarded by the State of Florida

What it is: State money provided for a specific program or project.

Who pays: It comes from taxes and other money collected by the state.

Why the County receives it: The County receives an award for work that meets state requirements.

For example: A grant may help pay for roads, environmental work, public safety, or community programs.

Visitor Sales Taxes$38.8MVisitors making taxable purchases here

What it is: The local share of sales taxes generated when visitors buy taxable goods.

Who pays: Visitors shopping, dining, and making other taxable purchases in Walton County.

Why the County receives it: Part of locally generated sales-tax activity is returned to local government.

For example: Visitor spending adds money that can help support services used by residents and guests.

Other Outside Funding$1.5MPartner payments and government support

What it is: Money from other public agencies, agreements, reimbursements, or outside partners.

Who pays: Another government or organization.

Why the County receives it: The County may provide shared work or take part in a larger program.

For example: Another agency may reimburse Walton County for an eligible service or joint project.
