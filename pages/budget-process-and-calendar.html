Financial Structure, Policies, and Process

Budget Process & Calendar

A simplified guide to how Walton County develops, reviews, adopts, and manages the annual budget — and the key dates along the way.

The annual budget process turns department needs, revenue forecasts, public priorities, Board direction, and Florida statutory requirements into an adopted spending plan for the fiscal year.

View Budget Calendar Review Financial Policies

Four Phases, One Adopted Budget

Phase 1

Preparation

Departments prepare operating and capital requests based on service needs, staffing, projects, and funding assumptions.

Phase 2

Review

OMB and County Administration evaluate requests against revenue estimates, priorities, fund capacity, and policy guidance.

Phase 3

Workshops

The tentative budget is reviewed publicly with the Board, including funding requests, service levels, and the proposed millage rate.

Phase 4

Adoption

Required public hearings are held before the Board adopts the final millage rate and annual operating budget.

Who Does What

See the six roles in the budget processDepartments, OMB, Administration, Constitutional Officers, the Board, and residents each have a distinct part.

County departments

Evaluate service demand, staffing, equipment, operating needs, and capital projects, then prepare requests and explain the service results those resources are intended to support.

Office of Management and Budget

Issues budget instructions, develops revenue estimates, reviews requests, tests fund balance and policy impacts, and prepares recommendations and public budget materials.

County Administration

Reviews department requests and recommendations for operational feasibility, service priorities, organizational needs, and alignment with Board direction.

Constitutional Officers

Independently elected offices prepare and submit their budget requests under applicable law. The County incorporates the required funding into the countywide budget presented for adoption.

Board of County Commissioners

Reviews recommendations in public meetings, provides policy direction, establishes the proposed and final millage rates, and adopts the annual budget after the required hearings.

Residents and stakeholders

Review proposed materials, attend workshops and hearings, ask questions, identify service concerns, and provide recommendations before final adoption.

Basis of Budgeting

Walton County prepares and adopts its annual budget in accordance with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles and applicable Florida Statutes governing local government budgeting practices.

See the accounting standards behind the budgetGAAP, encumbrance accounting, modified accrual basis, and grant revenue treatment.
GAAP

Budgeting Standards

Annual budgets for governmental fund types that are legally required to prepare a budget are adopted on a basis consistent with GAAP.

Control

Encumbrance Accounting

Encumbrances represent commitments for goods or services, including purchase orders and contractual obligations.

Accounting

Modified Accrual Basis

The modified accrual basis is used for budgeting purposes and audited financial statements for governmental funds.

Grants

Grant Revenue Treatment

Grant revenue is generally added to the budget after grant agreements are executed or prior-year grant balances are carried forward.

What Happens During The Process

From requests to recommendations

Budget development begins when departments receive instructions from the Office of Management and Budget. Departments review current programs, service demands, staffing, capital needs, and operating requirements before submitting requests.

OMB reviews requests with County Administration and departments, compares proposed expenditures to available resources, and prepares recommendations for Board consideration.

  • Operating and capital requests are reviewed together.
  • Constitutional Officer budgets are incorporated at required points in the process.
  • Public workshops and hearings provide transparency before adoption.

Public Input & Decision Record

How to participate

Budget workshops and hearings are open to the public. The Budget Calendar lists key dates and participation information. Questions, concerns, and recommendations may also be sent to the Office of Management and Budget at budget@mywaltonfl.gov.

Board direction and formal action occur in public meetings. As the tentative budget changes, revised schedules and the Summary of Budget Changes and Adjustments provide the financial record, while official meeting records document the discussion and action.

How Community Priorities Reach the Budget

How priorities are identified

The Board of County Commissioners sets countywide direction through its adopted Strategic Priority Initiatives. Departments tie their goals, objectives, and performance measures to those initiatives, and the code shown beside each measure on a department page identifies which initiative it supports.

Review the Strategic Initiatives

See who is asked for input and how it reaches the budgetHow service-level information is gathered, who submits requests, and how the budget reflects those priorities.

Who is asked for input

Budget workshops and the two statutory TRIM hearings are open to all residents, property owners, and businesses, and are noticed in advance through the newspaper advertisement, the County meeting portal, and the County news subscription list. Every County department and every independently elected Constitutional Officer submits a request. Outside agencies that receive County funding — volunteer fire departments, health and human-services providers, and economic-development organizations — submit written requests that are published with the budget.

Input is gathered through the statutory public process rather than through a countywide opinion survey. The County did not field a resident budget survey for FY 2027, and it does not treat the workshops as a substitute for one: the record of what was raised and what changed as a result is published above, so residents can see the effect of participation rather than being asked to assume it.

See the submitted agency requests

How the budget reflects those priorities

Each department page shows the services the department provides, the resources proposed for it, and, where measures have been submitted, the targets it expects to reach. The Transmittal Letter explains the six conditions that shaped this proposal and the response to each.

Service-level information also reaches the budget through department requests, permitting and development activity, capital needs identified by Engineering and Public Works, constitutional-officer submissions, and issues raised by residents at Board meetings throughout the year. Where the Board changes the proposal, the change is recorded above and carried into the revised schedules and the Summary of Budget Changes and Adjustments.

What Departments Were Asked to Submit

See the budget instructions, assumptions, and advisory structureWhat departments were told to submit, the cost assumptions they were given, and who advises the budget.

Budget instructions

The Office of Management and Budget issues written budget instructions that open the annual process. Departments are asked to build their request against current service levels, identify personnel changes by position, separate recurring operating costs from one-time needs, and submit capital and equipment requests with justification.

For FY 2027 departments were also asked to complete a budget-reduction exercise, identifying what they would reduce if resources were constrained, so that the Board could weigh trade-offs at the workshops.

The budget instruction memorandum itself, its issue date, and the specific reduction target departments were given are not published on this site. Posting the instructions with the supporting documentation would answer this question directly.

Assumptions departments were given

So that requests are comparable, OMB supplies the cost assumptions departments must apply rather than letting each department choose its own. For FY 2027 these include a 3% cost-of-living adjustment, the published health-plan rates by plan and coverage tier, statutory FICA and Medicare rates, and Florida Retirement System contribution rates by pension class.

Who advises the budget

Walton County does not use volunteer advisory bodies, a citizen budget committee, or a task force convened to prepare the budget. The work is done by permanent County staff and decided by the five directly elected members of the Board of County Commissioners.

That is a deliberate structure rather than a gap. Florida’s Government-in-the-Sunshine Law and the Truth in Millage process require these deliberations to take place in noticed public meetings, on a published schedule, with the proposed tax rate advertised to every property owner before it can be adopted. Responsibility for the budget stays with officials residents elect directly and can replace, and every decision point is open to the public at the time it is made rather than after an appointed body has met.

The budget does rely on independent outside sources rather than County judgment alone. Revenue estimates use Florida Office of Economic and Demographic Research distributions, the Property Appraiser’s certified taxable values, and state revenue-sharing estimates. Personnel costs use Florida Retirement System contribution rates and carrier health-plan rates. Constitutional Officers prepare and submit their own budgets independently under Florida law, and outside agencies that receive County funding submit written requests that are published with this budget.

Budget Implementation & Amendments

See how the budget is monitored and amended after adoptionOngoing monitoring and the statutory process for amending the approved budget.

Ongoing budget monitoring

After adoption, the budget remains an active financial management tool. The Office of Management and Budget and County departments monitor spending, revenues, appropriations, and amendments throughout the fiscal year.

Budgetary control is established at the fund level for all funds to ensure total fund appropriations remain within approved limits.

Budget amendment process

When circumstances require changes to the approved budget, the Board of County Commissioners may amend the budget by resolution in accordance with Section 129.06, Florida Statutes.

Amendments may be needed for unforeseen expenditures, use of contingency reserves, allocation of unanticipated revenues, grant revenues, or carry-forward funding.
