Transmittal Letter

A budget message for Walton County.

The transmittal letter introduces the annual budget, explains the financial conditions shaping the plan, and connects the County’s priorities to the services, infrastructure, and stewardship residents depend on.

To the Honorable Walton County Board of County Commissioners:

In accordance with Florida Statute Chapter 129.03(3), I am transmitting with this message the budget for Fiscal Year 2027. This budget is balanced and provides a general operating framework for providing all County services for the forthcoming year. This budget focuses on public safety, infrastructure needs, and providing services for a continually growing county in the most efficient and effective manner possible.

The budget has been developed with the following objectives:

  • Maintaining the existing service levels for residents.
  • Consideration of the Board of County Commissioners’ directives.
  • Funding the Sheriff, other Constitutional Officers, and other statutory requirements.
  • Addressing the needs required for an aging infrastructure and the maintenance of county facilities.
  • Investing in our employees.
  • Maintaining a sound self-insurance fund for health insurance.
  • Maintaining reserves.

Summary of Changes

The Fiscal Year 2027 Final Budget is $2,935,920 higher than the Tentative Budget. This includes an amount of excess EMS revenue not published here requested to be budgeted by the Sheriff’s Office, and a net increase of $1,235,920 in other appropriations. In the General Fund, interfund transfers-in increased, reflecting a Clerk of Court IT staffing request not published here and a cost-of-living adjustment amount for the Clerk of Court not published here, as recommended by the BCC at the proposed workshop. In the Transportation Fund, a cost-of-living adjustment amount not published here was offset by increased state contributions. The Debt Service Fund now includes a remaining cash balance amount not published here, representing the remaining cash balance from the voter-approved sales tax referendum dedicated to the construction of the Highway 331 bridge. The largest change occurred in the Fine & Forfeiture Fund, an increase of $4.7 million, including an EMS excess revenue amount not published here for capital recreation projects and an amount not published here redirected to the Bruce Fire Station project. This increase was offset by a $2.5 million increase in interest revenue.

In comparing the FY 27 Budget to the FY 26 Final Budget, the total budget across all funds has increased from $327,945,088 to $364,932,816. This difference is due to a combination of several factors. Staff’s primary focus has been on meeting infrastructure needs while maintaining current service levels for our citizens. With this goal, an additional $10 million has been budgeted within the Capital Projects Fund for various infrastructure projects. Within the General Fund’s discretion, a Managed Vendor Program revenue amount not published here has also been budgeted toward infrastructure improvements. A Road/Bridge/Mossy Head infrastructure allocation amount not published here has also been allocated to the Mossy Head Community Redevelopment Agency, along with an additional funding request from the Walton County Health Department to maintain current primary care services within Walton County.

General Government Taxes remain the county’s largest revenue source, accounting for 73% of budgeted revenues and projected at $265,247,494, with Ad Valorem Taxes making up 44%, or $161,066,332, of the total. The projection for Ad Valorem taxes is supported by an increase in the County-Wide Certified Taxable Value (the prior fiscal year comparison value is not published here) for Fiscal Year 2027. The Discretionary Sales Surtax now contributes 11%, or $40,000,000, to be utilized primarily for solid waste collection and disposal, as well as funding road and bridge construction and maintenance.

Intergovernmental Revenues, expected to rise slightly to $29,662,519, include all revenues received from federal and state government sources. These revenues comprise 8.7% of budgeted revenues.

Miscellaneous Revenues are anticipated to increase to $38,681,133, primarily from increased budgeted interest earnings and an increase in expected Recreation Plat Fee collections. Charges for Services are projected to generate $14,642,264, reflecting an increase from the prior year’s budget, in line with the sustained demand for county services. Permits, Fees, and Special Assessments are budgeted at $3,400,000, a decline from the prior year.

Budget Summary

The final budget has been crafted with a countywide operating millage rate of 3.5190 mills and a North Walton Mosquito Control millage rate of 0.4410 mills.

Public Safety remains the largest expenditure category for our County, with an allocation of $126,571,918, staying relatively flat compared to the prior year. The Sheriff’s Office allocation of $114,116,228 reflects a slight increase from the prior year. Of the total public-safety expenditure, 90% is allocated to the Walton County Sheriff’s Office, while the remaining 10% funds other services such as the Building Department, Code Enforcement, and the Medical Examiner.

General Government expenditures reflect a decrease from the previous year, ensuring continued support for essential government functions, including the constitutional offices. The Economic Environment category is budgeted at $62,761,100, a decrease from the prior year due to lower Tourist Development Tax collections seen during FY 26. Additionally, this reflects a decrease in overall expenses related to solid waste collection and disposal.

The budget for the Physical Environment activity category has been increased to $24,559,033, reflecting an increase in overall expenses related to solid waste collection and disposal. Culture and Recreation funding has been budgeted at $6,151,031, or a moderate increase in anticipated recreation program costs, and an increase in Parks Maintenance staffing and expenditures related to maintenance of community parks facilities. The Human Services activity category has increased, driven by increased funding for the DeFuniak Community Redevelopment Agency, and an additional funding request from the Walton County Health Department to maintain current primary care services within Walton County.

Opportunities and Challenges in Budget Development

As we move through the budget process and into the upcoming budget year, we are aware that there are several factors that could potentially impact government operations over the next year. There is a possible impact on revenues due to legislative changes with sales and property taxes. Another long-term concern is the uncertainty of future economic conditions, while both tourism development taxes are collected and used within that fund for eligible projects as allowable under Florida statute related to tourism, sales tax revenues collected in Solid Waste and General Fund are impacted by tourism levels within the County. The budget development process presents both challenges and opportunities that will strengthen our financial planning and resource allocation for the future. As we move through the remainder of the current fiscal year and into the next fiscal year, it will be imperative to closely monitor those and other revenue sources that the County relies on for revenue.

The county’s reliance on sales tax revenue is particularly significant, given that a large portion of all consumer purchases in Walton County are taxed. Because the half-cent and one-cent sales tax revenues, though projected to remain moderately flat this year, can continue to rise and fall with the strength of the local economy, focusing on optimizing our financial management practices, we can continue to support essential services and infrastructure projects without compromising the quality of life for our residents. Similarly, although tourism development taxes are expected to experience a decline in recent years, this is a natural rebalancing as global travel has normalized post-pandemic. The dip offers us the chance to refine our tourism strategies and focus on attracting visitors who will continue to contribute positively to the local economy.

Overall, while there are challenges ahead, they also offer opportunities for growth, innovation, and improved financial management. By tackling these challenges with a proactive and strategic approach, we can ensure that our community remains well-equipped to meet the challenges of the upcoming fiscal year while continuing to provide the high level of service our residents expect.

Full-Time Positions

The final budget includes additional FTEs under the Board of County Commissioners, and an FTE count not published here for the Clerk of Courts, and an FTE count not published here for the Supervisor of Elections. This requests focus on the need to maintain existing county facilities and levels of service. A detailed personnel summary is provided on each department budget page in this document, and an overall summary is provided as a separate page within the budget document.

During the budget process, each department identifies the positions that are necessary to either provide an enhanced level of service or maintain an existing one. Not all requests for new positions were included in the FY 2027 Tentative Budget, with some requests not being recommended. Positions that were not recommended were those that added new services or were considered an expansion of existing service. Only the BCC has the ability to add positions; additional positions approved or denied by the BCC during the budget workshops will be added or removed accordingly and reflected in the staffing totals.

New positions approved for FY 27 are as follows:

  • Facilities Maintenance: FTEs for a construction crew, unrelated to a neutral budget item, smaller projects previously contracted will be done in-house.
  • Custodial: FTEs for increased facilities and workload, with one FTE dedicated to the Building Department facility and the cost recouped through the cost allocation study.
  • Parks Maintenance: FTEs for increased workload with park expansions and aging park facilities.
  • Public Works: FTEs for a Utility Inspector and a Stabilization Crew.
  • Office of Management and Budget: an FTE for an experienced Budget Analyst for succession planning.
  • Procurement: an FTE for additional duties related to contracts awarded for solicitation.
  • Mossy Head Wastewater Treatment Facility: an FTE bringing the Sewer Technician to a full FTE.

These positions were partially offset by reductions in Engineering, Eagle Springs Grill, Building Department, and Section 8 Housing. The Clerk of Courts & Comptroller has requested an FTE for IT Network and an FTE for Payroll Specialist. The Supervisor of Elections has requested an FTE for a part-time employee for the South Walton Office.

These positions were partially offset by reductions elsewhere. By making these adjustments, we are ensuring that our County remains well-equipped to meet the operational needs and strategic objectives of our residents while continuing to provide the high level of service our residents deserve.

Major Fund Highlights

As we transition into Fiscal Year 2027, the General Fund budget has been tentatively set at $81,424,611, a notable increase from the allocation in FY 26. The largest increase in revenue is attributed to a 9.70% increase in proposed taxable property values over the prior year, resulting in a corresponding increase in tentative ad valorem tax revenues. The transfer of these ad valorem tax revenues from the General Fund to the Capital Projects and Fine & Forfeiture Funds throughout the year. The other main driver of the increase in the General Fund budget is the addition of the Managed Vendor Program revenue and expenditures.

The Transportation Fund for Fiscal Year 2027 is budgeted at $26,723,118, a modest increase from the allocated in FY 26. This investment supports the county’s commitment to maintaining and improving its transportation infrastructure, which is crucial for both residents and the local economy. Projected costs have remained relatively flat for FY 27 due to a lower anticipated cost of capital projects to be completed in the coming fiscal year.

The Fine & Forfeiture Fund budget for Fiscal Year 2027 is tentatively set at $114,116,228, an increase over last year’s budget of $114,116,228. The Fiscal Year 2027 Budget includes an EMS excess revenue amount not published here to be requested by the Sheriff’s Office and an amount not published here redirected to the Bruce Fire Station, contributing to the overall increase in this fund.

The Tourist Development Fund for Fiscal Year 2027 is tentatively set at $58,965,950, reflecting a decrease from the $51,500,000 budgeted in FY 26. This tentative decrease is due to Tourist Development Tax revenues remaining flat compared to the prior year figure not published here. This budget has been approved and recommended by the Tourist Development Council, which focused on conservative budgeting principles and an increased effort for marketing for FY 27.

The Solid Waste Fund for Fiscal Year 2027 is at $23,119,567, reflecting an increase from FY 26. In the Solid Waste Fund budget focus on regular operations and maintenance of the county’s solid waste management system, as well as an increase in transfers out to the Transportation Fund for road and bridge projects.

The Capital Project Fund for Fiscal Year 2027 is tentatively set at $27,586,536, marking a substantial increase from the allocated in FY 26. This increase in over reflects the county’s commitment to advancing critical infrastructure projects that are essential for supporting the community’s growth and development.

In summary, the Fiscal Year 2027 budget reflects a balanced and forward-thinking approach, carefully constructed to meet the immediate needs of our community while positioning Walton County for sustained growth and resilience. This budget reflects our commitment to fiscal responsibility, strategic investment in critical infrastructure, and the continued delivery of high-quality services to our residents. Together, we are ensuring that Walton County remains a vibrant and prosperous place to live, work, and grow.

Melissa Thomason,
Chief Financial Officer

Challenges, Opportunities, and Decisions

The conditions behind this proposal.

These conditions were identified through department operating and capital requests, revenue and taxable-value estimates, constitutional-officer submissions, the five-year financial forecast, Board budget workshops, and the public budget process. They are not listed as a ranking of community preferences; they explain the major pressures and opportunities considered while preparing the tentative budget. Because the budget remains tentative until final adoption, public comments and Board decisions may change the plan.

1

Maintain services at the rolled-back millage rate

The tentative countywide millage rate equals the rolled-back rate. This limits revenue growth from the existing property-tax base while operating costs and service demands continue to change.

Root condition
Florida’s rolled-back-rate calculation reflects taxable-value growth while excluding most new-construction value. The tentative rate is designed to produce approximately the same countywide levy from the prior-year tax base, before new construction.
Planning horizon
Immediate FY 2027 adoption decision with effects carried through the five-year financial forecast.
Community and service effect
Countywide property owners and residents are affected through the tax rate and the service capacity supported by recurring revenue.
Budget response
Departments and Administration reviewed requests against recurring revenue, fund capacity, and a required budget-reduction exercise.
How progress is measured
Adoption of a balanced budget, recurring revenues compared with recurring expenditures, and projected fund-balance trends.
Property tax detailsBudget changes
2

Fund public safety while preserving other County services

The independently elected Sheriff’s budget is supported by the General Fund after applying revenue available within the Sheriff Fund. That funding requirement competes with the resources used for Board-controlled departments and other countywide obligations.

Root condition
The Sheriff is independently elected, while the Board provides the transfer required after revenue available within the Sheriff Fund is applied.
Planning horizon
Annual appropriation decision evaluated across the five-year General Fund and Sheriff Fund forecasts.
Community and service effect
Public-safety funding serves the countywide population and reduces resources otherwise available for Board departments and other county obligations.
Budget response
The forecast treats the Sheriff Fund as transfer supported and calculates the General Fund transfer needed after the Sheriff’s other projected revenues.
How progress is measured
Annual transfer requirements, General Fund structural balance, Sheriff service measures, and public-safety funding compared with the adopted plan.
Sheriff’s OfficeInterfund transfers
3

Deliver infrastructure for a growing county

Roads, drainage, public facilities, recreation assets, public-safety facilities, and other infrastructure often require several years of planning, design, funding, and construction.

Root condition
Population and development growth, aging assets, permitting, land acquisition, and multiyear design and construction schedules create long-lived infrastructure needs.
Planning horizon
Five-year capital plan, with some project life cycles extending beyond the plan period.
Community and service effect
Effects vary by project location and service area, while countywide residents and visitors benefit from the broader transportation, safety, drainage, and facility network.
Budget response
The five-year Capital Improvement Plan organizes projects by timing and funding source and identifies the departments responsible for delivery.
How progress is measured
Project status, planned versus actual timing, available funding, completion of scheduled phases, and the service benefit delivered.
Capital planSearch projects
4

Keep recurring costs aligned with sustainable revenue

Personnel, contracts, utilities, maintenance, and other operating costs continue beyond a single budget year. One-time resources can support one-time needs but should not hide an ongoing operating imbalance.

Root condition
Continuing personnel, contractual, utility, maintenance, and service costs can grow differently from recurring tax and fee revenue.
Planning horizon
FY 2027 operating plan tested through FY 2031.
Community and service effect
A persistent mismatch would reduce future service capacity or require later revenue, spending, or fund-balance decisions.
Budget response
The five-year forecast applies documented assumptions to recurring revenue and expenditure lines and excludes unusual one-time activity from trend calculations when it would distort the outlook.
How progress is measured
Annual revenue compared with expenditure, changes in available fund balance, and updates to assumptions as actual results become available.
Financial forecastRevenue sources
5

Protect committed and restricted resources

Fund balance is not a single pool of money available for any purpose. Legal restrictions, revenue-source limitations, and Board commitments determine how portions of a balance may be used.

Root condition
Florida law, revenue-source requirements, fund accounting, and formal Board commitments limit how certain resources may be used.
Planning horizon
Multiyear stewardship extending beyond a single annual budget.
Community and service effect
Resources remain available only for their authorized or committed purpose and cannot automatically offset unrelated services.
Budget response
The forecast separately identifies the Board-committed Tourist Development balance for Beach Renourishment and the balance available for other eligible Tourist Development uses.
How progress is measured
Fund-balance classifications, compliance with financial policies, and the availability of resources for their authorized purposes.
Fund schedulesFinancial policies
6

Use dedicated and outside revenue effectively

Tourist Development Taxes, fuel taxes, grants, impact fees, special assessments, and other dedicated sources can reduce pressure on general revenue, but their use may be limited and their timing or amount may be less predictable.

Root condition
Collections depend on economic activity, visitor spending, fuel use, development, grant eligibility, intergovernmental formulas, and other factors outside direct County control.
Planning horizon
Annual estimates monitored against a five-year revenue outlook and project schedule.
Community and service effect
Who pays and who benefits varies by source: property owners, visitors, motorists, service users, developers, and outside governments support different funds and eligible services.
Budget response
Revenue is budgeted in the funds and service areas permitted by each source, while grant-funded and other one-time capital activity is presented separately where practicable.
How progress is measured
Collections compared with estimates, compliance with restrictions, grant and project delivery, and the share of eligible costs supported by dedicated revenue.
