Reader Guide

How to read the revenue plan

Revenue sources differ in reliability, legal use, who pays them, and how much control the County has over the amount collected. Those differences matter when deciding which revenues can support continuing services.

Recurring operating revenue

Ongoing property taxes, state-shared distributions, and recurring charges or fees may support continuing services when the underlying activity and collection pattern are expected to continue.

Dedicated or restricted revenue

Tourist Development Taxes, fuel taxes, impact fees, special assessments, and grants may be limited to particular purposes, projects, service areas, or eligible costs.

Variable or nonrecurring resources

Grants, debt proceeds, asset sales, contributions, and use of fund balance can change significantly and generally should not support an ongoing service unless a reliable continuing source is identified.

County control and outside risk

The Board sets the countywide millage rate within Florida law and approves certain rates and fees. State formulas, grants, economic activity, tourism, and fuel consumption are not fully controlled by the County.

Who pays

Property owners support ad valorem taxes; visitors contribute Tourist Development Taxes; motorists generate fuel-tax revenue; service users pay applicable charges; and federal or state governments provide grants and shared revenue.

FY 2027 revenue decisions

The tentative countywide millage rate is set at the rolled-back rate. North Walton Mosquito Control has a separate millage rate. Review the tax rates, levy, and departmental allocations in the property tax summary.

Rate and fee authority

The Board approves the County millage rates and certain County fees within applicable law. Other amounts are established through state formulas, grant awards, contracts, interlocal agreements, market activity, or action by another government.

Revenue burden and cost recovery

The current budget identifies the general type of payer for major sources, but it does not contain verified resident, nonresident, household, or business burden shares or service-level cost-recovery targets. Those measures should be added only when a documented methodology and source are available.

Important: These are general planning characteristics. The legal use and reliability of a revenue source must be evaluated at the individual fund and account level. Interfund transfers may be recurring or nonrecurring depending on their source and purpose. Detailed forecast assumptions are available in the Financial Forecast.

Who owns the property tax base

Homes carry most of the property tax base; exemptions remove about $3.2 billion from what is taxed.

The County does not track ad valorem collections by sector directly. The closest publicly available measure is the Florida Department of Revenue’s published breakdown of each county’s real property just value by classification, used here as a value-based proxy for who carries the property tax base.

What this shows: Single-family, multi-family, and vacant residential parcels made up 87.9% of Walton County’s $57.1 billion in real property just value for 2024. Commercial and industrial parcels, developed and vacant, made up 5.3%. Because the countywide millage rate applies uniformly to taxable value, residential property supports a similarly large share of ad valorem tax revenue — this is a property-value proxy, not a direct measure of dollars collected by sector.

Value of exemptions: $3,212,567,104 in assessed value was exempt from County ad valorem taxation in 2024 (chiefly homestead exemptions), out of $53.1 billion in total county assessed value. At the 2024 county operating millage of 3.6000 mills, that represents approximately $11.6 million in County ad valorem revenue foregone through exemptions that year. The County does not calculate this figure itself; it is derived here from the Department of Revenue’s published totals and is not a FY 2027 estimate.

Source: Florida Department of Revenue, Walton County Property Tax Overview (2024), the most recent certified county profile published. Figures reflect calendar-year 2024 tax-roll data; FY 2027 uses updated taxable values not yet reflected in this statewide report.

Choices available to the County

How could Walton County increase revenue?

These are available policy tools, not recommendations and not assumptions in the FY 2027 tentative budget. Each choice affects different taxpayers, may be restricted to specific uses, and requires the public process and approval established by Florida law.

01

Increase the county millage rate

The Board could adopt a higher operating millage within constitutional and statutory limits, increasing property taxes. Florida’s TRIM process requires public notice, hearings, and specified Board voting thresholds depending on the rate selected.

Property owners
02

Increase local-option fuel taxes

The County could levy additional authorized cents per gallon that are not currently imposed, subject to Florida law and the required ordinance or interlocal process. Fuel-tax proceeds are restricted to eligible transportation purposes.

Motorists
03

Increase or expand Tourist Development Taxes in North Walton

The North Walton district currently has a lower transient-rental tax rate than South Walton. The County could pursue additional eligible Tourist Development Tax levies in that district. The exact levy, approval method, referendum requirement, and permitted uses depend on the statutory tax component considered.

Short-term lodging guests
04

Adjust charges and fees for services

The Board could revise legally authorized charges for recreation programs and facilities, golf, wastewater, solid waste, permits, development review, records, rentals, and optional or nonresident library services. Fees should be connected to the service provided and reviewed for affordability, access, and cost recovery.

Service users
05

Adopt or revise special assessments and MSBUs

The County may charge benefiting properties for eligible services or improvements such as roads, lighting, fire protection, drainage, or other local benefits when legal benefit and apportionment requirements are met.

Benefiting properties
06

Update impact fees

Impact fees may be adjusted when a current study demonstrates that new development creates a proportional need for additional capital facilities. The money is restricted to eligible growth-related capital costs, not general operations.

New development
07

Seek a discretionary sales surtax

Where authorized by Florida law, the County could place a local discretionary sales surtax before voters for an eligible purpose. This generally requires a referendum and may carry expenditure-plan, duration, and sharing requirements.

Residents and visitors making taxable purchases
08

Pursue grants, shared revenues, and partnerships

The County can seek competitive grants, legislative appropriations, intergovernmental agreements, and partner contributions. These sources reduce local funding needs but are often restricted, competitive, temporary, or require a County match.

Other governments and partners
09

Use financing for eligible capital projects

Bonds and loans can make capital funding available sooner, but they are financing—not new recurring revenue—and commit future revenue to principal and interest. Debt must follow County policy and applicable approval requirements.
