Select a question to see how departments, programs, and staffing translate into spending.

What services does each department provide?

Every department has its own service narrative.

Each of the County’s 78 departments and agencies has a page describing what it does, its core services, and what it costs. That’s the level department budgets are built and reported at — not a single lump sum.

What are the costs for each department?

Object-level detail, down to the transaction.

Department pages break spending down by object code — personnel, operating, capital — with drill-down into the underlying transactions, so a total can be traced back to what it actually paid for.

How are costs measured?

By object code and object type.

Every dollar is classified into a standard object code and type — the same classification system used across every department, so spending can be compared consistently department to department.

What are costs for contracted purchases?

Tracked separately, with vendor detail.

Contracted purchases are reported with the vendor and contract linked, so residents can see who the County pays and for what, not just a total labeled “contractual services.”

How is a department held accountable for results?

Oversight, monitoring, and public reporting.

Each department page states how it is overseen, how its budget is monitored during the year, and what performance measures and targets are available for that department’s work.

Does a department work with other departments?

Most services depend on shared work.

Each department page identifies the other departments and shared processes it relies on to deliver its services — spending rarely happens in isolation from the rest of County government.

What are the major programs and services?

Eleven program areas group the work.

Beyond individual departments, spending is also grouped into 11 broader program areas spanning Board departments, Constitutional Officers, and other autonomous entities — a different, complementary way to see where money goes.

What are the costs for each major program?

Shown by year-over-year change and cost type.

Each program shows its year-over-year budget and staffing change, plus a cost split across personnel, operating, capital, debt, and grants — so the shape of the spending is visible, not just the total.

Do programs and services generate revenue?

Some are self-funded; most are subsidized.

Each program discloses the dollar amount and share of its cost not covered by revenue assigned directly to it — calculated net of interfund transfers, so a transfer-supported program isn’t mistaken for self-funded.

How do programs align with County priorities?

Strategic Initiative codes make the link explicit.

Where a department has performance measures, they carry a code tying that work back to one of the Board’s adopted Strategic Priority Initiatives.

How many total staff are budgeted?

Reported by office and department.

Full-time-equivalent staffing is reported by office and department for FY 2024 through FY 2027, so staffing levels can be tracked over time, not just for the current year.

Did personnel costs change from last year?

Yes, and it’s shown with prior-year columns.

Personnel cost figures are shown alongside prior-year amounts, so the change — and what’s driving it, like cost-of-living adjustments or health-insurance rates — is visible rather than buried in a single-year number.

Where were staffing levels increased or reduced?

Tracked department by department.

A department-level change column and staffing notes show exactly where positions were added or reduced, rather than only a single countywide staffing total.

What’s the difference between “people” and “place” spending?

Two ways of grouping the same budget.

“Caring for Our People” groups services focused on safety, health, and residents’ daily lives. “Caring for Our Place” groups services focused on infrastructure, shared systems, and the natural environment. Together they cover the full FY 2027 operating budget, just organized around outcomes instead of department names.

Are costs ever shared between departments?

Yes, through interfund transfers.

Some funding moves between County funds before it reaches a department — most visibly the General Fund transfer that supports the Sheriff’s Office. Those transfers are tracked separately so they aren’t double-counted in departments’ own totals.
