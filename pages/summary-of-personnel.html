Full-time, part-time and scope

A full-time equivalent, or FTE, is a way of counting budgeted work rather than people. Two people each working half the year count as one FTE, and an authorized position that sits vacant still counts. It is a measure of what the budget pays for, not a headcount of who is at a desk today.

How does the County decide to add a job?

A written justification

Reviewed and approved by County Administration before the position can enter the tentative budget.

How the review works

A department cannot add a position simply by including it in its request. The department must submit a written staffing justification explaining the service need the position addresses, and County Administration reviews and approves it before the position enters the tentative budget. Requests that are not approved never reach the Board.

That is why authorized FTE moves by small amounts in most departments: each additional position has been argued for individually rather than applied as an across-the-board allowance.

Is County pay competitive?

15.6% below market

Measured at the midpoint against 20 peer counties and cities in an independent 2023 study.

What the study found

An independent Compensation and Classification Study by Evergreen Solutions, presented to the Board in July 2023, benchmarked 78 County job classifications against 20 peer employers. County pay ranges were 21.8% below the market at the minimum, 15.6% below at the midpoint, and 5.2% below at the maximum.

The County responded by adopting revised pay plans built to be competitive at the middle of the market. See the full comparison and what it costs to close the gap, or read the study.

Merit increases and timing

The tentative budget includes a 3% cost-of-living adjustment, and that is the only general increase planned for FY 2027.

Separately, a department director may work with Human Resources and County Administration to award a merit increase to an employee whose performance goes above and beyond what the job requires. These are individual awards made under the County’s established pay policy, not a scheduled increase applied to a group.

How County pay compares to the market

Walton County commissioned an independent Compensation and Classification Study from Evergreen Solutions, LLC, presented to the Board on July 5, 2023. It is the reference point the County uses to judge whether its pay is keeping pace with what comparable employers offer, and it is the reason the pay plan looks the way it does today.

What the study found

Measured against the 50th percentile of the market, County pay ranges sat 21.8% below at the minimum, 15.6% below at the midpoint, and 5.2% below at the maximum. Most employees were paid in the lower half of their own range: 70.7% fell below the midpoint of the grade they were assigned to.

The study also identified salary compression. Roughly 14.5% of employees earned within 20% of their supervisor’s salary, and 3.8% earned more than their supervisor.

Who the County was compared against

Peers included the neighbouring and regionally comparable counties of Escambia, Santa Rosa, Okaloosa, Bay, Holmes, Washington, Monroe, Gulf, Putnam, Nassau and Hernando, along with Baldwin County, Alabama.

Because the County competes for staff with nearby municipalities as well as other counties, the survey also covered the cities of DeFuniak Springs, Destin, Fort Walton Beach, Panama City, Panama City Beach, Pensacola, Crestview and Niceville.

What the County did about it

The study produced revised non-exempt and exempt pay plans built to be competitive at the 50th percentile, with consistent grade progression and a 65% range spread, and a reduced number of steps in the non-exempt plan.

Evergreen costed four ways of moving employees onto the new plans, ranging from $1.65 million to bring everyone to the new grade minimum up to $2.60 million for full hire-year parity. The blended approach — hybrid-year placement for non-exempt staff and class-year parity for exempt staff — was costed at $2.27 million, affecting 519 employees at an average adjustment of 8.76%.

How employees were involved

The study was not built from payroll records alone. All employees were surveyed in January 2023 and 211 responded, and on-site interviews and focus groups were held across departments and management levels.

Every classification was scored through a Job Assessment Tool on five factors — leadership, working conditions, complexity, decision making, and relationships — with employee input, supervisor review, and separate review by the consultant and Human Resources.

Keeping the comparison current. Evergreen recommended that the County run small-scale salary surveys as needed for hard-to-fill classifications and repeat a comprehensive study every three to five years. On that schedule the next comprehensive study falls due between 2026 and 2028, and this budget does not state whether one is planned or funded. Confirm the County’s intent and record it here, together with which implementation option the Board adopted and how much of it has been funded to date.
