How are budget decisions made?

Through a structured public process required by Florida law. The budget moves through four phases — Preparation, Review, Workshops, and Adoption — beginning around February and ending in September with the Board of County Commissioners’ legal adoption of a final millage rate and budget.

Who starts the budget process?

Departments and the Office of Management and Budget.

Departments receive instructions from OMB and prepare operating and capital requests based on service needs, staffing, projects, and funding assumptions. OMB issues the budget instructions that open the annual process.

How are requests reviewed?

OMB and County Administration evaluate each request.

Requests are tested against revenue estimates, County priorities, fund capacity, and policy guidance. Staff compares proposed expenditures to available resources and prepares recommendations for the Board.

Can the public see the budget before it is adopted?

Yes. Public workshops are held before adoption.

The tentative budget is reviewed publicly with the Board, including funding requests, service levels, and the proposed millage rate. Residents can attend workshops, ask questions, and provide input before final decisions.

Who makes the final decision?

The Board of County Commissioners, after public hearings.

Florida law requires public hearings before the Board adopts the final millage rate and annual operating budget. The five elected Commissioners vote on the budget that becomes the fiscal year’s spending plan.

What rules does the County have to follow?

Florida Statutes and the TRIM law govern the process.

Chapters 125, 129, 200, and 218 of Florida Statutes govern the county budget process. The Truth in Millage law requires formally noticed public hearings and a tax-rate advertisement before final adoption.

Can the budget change after it is adopted?

Yes, but only through a formal process.

After adoption, the Board may amend the budget by resolution under Section 129.06, Florida Statutes, to address unforeseen expenditures, contingency needs, unanticipated revenue, grants, or carry-forward funding. The budget remains an active management tool throughout the year.

How does a request become part of the budget?

  1. PrepareDepartments submit operating and capital requests to OMB.
  2. ReviewOMB and Administration evaluate requests against revenue and priorities.
  3. WorkshopsThe Board reviews the tentative budget in public meetings.
  4. HearingsFlorida law requires public hearings before final adoption.
  5. AdoptThe Board adopts the final millage rate and annual budget.

The budget is decided in public: from department requests to final adoption, every major step is open to residents, and the final vote happens only after legally required hearings.
