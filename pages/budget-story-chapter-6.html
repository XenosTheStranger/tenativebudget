Why does the budget change?

Because the community the budget serves is always changing. Population growth, inflation, new development, tourism shifts, state legislation, emergencies, grants, and changing community priorities all shape the budget from year to year. A budget that never moved would slowly stop matching the needs it is meant to serve.

Can revenue collections change the budget?

Yes. Money in changes what can go out.

When a revenue source like the Tourist Development Tax or sales surtax collects more or less than projected, the funding available in the related fund shifts accordingly. Revenue that outperforms projections can support additional services or reserves; revenue that underperforms may require spending reductions or fund-balance use.

Do personnel and operating costs stay the same?

No. Prices for people and supplies move over time.

Personnel costs shift with Board-approved compensation changes, health-insurance premiums, and state-mandated Florida Retirement System contribution rates. Operating costs move with inflation in utilities, fuel, contracts, and supplies. These are recurring costs that continue beyond a single year.

Why does a single year look unusual sometimes?

One-time items can distort the picture.

A grant award, a capital project entering active construction, or a one-time asset sale can make a single year’s figure much larger or smaller than a typical year. This does not indicate an ongoing trend — it is why distinguishing recurring from non-recurring revenue and expenditure matters when comparing years.

Can the state or federal government add costs?

Yes. New mandates can add obligations.

New state or federal requirements — unfunded or partially funded mandates — can add cost obligations the County did not previously carry. These changes may arrive mid-year or between budget cycles and can affect multiple departments or funds.

Can the Board change the budget before adoption?

Yes. Elected leaders set priorities in public.

During the public workshop phase of the annual budget process, the Board of County Commissioners can add, reduce, or reprioritize funding requests based on public input and policy direction. These decisions are made in open meetings before final adoption.

Can the budget change after it is adopted?

Yes, but only through a legal process.

After adoption, the Board may amend the budget by resolution under Section 129.06, Florida Statutes, to address unforeseen expenditures, use of contingency reserves, unanticipated revenue, new grants, or carry-forward funding. Amendments follow a specific statutory process, not informal adjustments.

How does a change reach the budget?

  1. Condition changesRevenue collections, costs, mandates, emergencies, or community needs shift.
  2. Need is identifiedDepartments, OMB, or the Board recognize the change and its budget effect.
  3. Options are weighedStaff tests the change against available resources, fund capacity, and policy.
  4. Board acts in publicThe Board considers the change in a workshop, hearing, or formal amendment.
  5. Budget is updatedThe adopted or amended figure replaces the prior plan in the public record.

Some variance is normal: because Actual figures reflect what truly happened and Budget figures reflect a plan made months in advance, some year-over-year difference is simply the expected gap between a forecast and reality — not evidence that either figure was wrong.
