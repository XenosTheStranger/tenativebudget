Chapter 1

What does the county do for me?

Long before anyone talks about budgets or taxes, the county is already part of your day — in the road you drove in on, the deputy patrolling your street, and the park down the block.

Why does the county need a budget?

Every service in Chapter 1 takes people, equipment, buildings, infrastructure, and long-term planning to deliver. The budget is simply the county’s financial plan for making all of that possible.

Coming Next Where does the money come from?
03
Chapter 3 · Preview

Where does the money come from?

County government is funded through many different sources — not just property taxes. Some revenue is legally restricted to a single purpose, while other revenue is more flexible and can support a wider range of services.

Coming Next How is my money used?
04
Chapter 4 · Preview

How is my money used?

Revenue becomes public services: the same funding streams from Chapter 3 pay for the deputies, roads, parks, and libraries you rely on every day — no accounting degree required.

Coming Next How are budget decisions made?
05
Chapter 5 · Preview

How are budget decisions made?

The budget is the result of a structured public process — department requests, staff recommendations, public workshops, and final decisions made by the Board of County Commissioners in the open.

Coming Next Why does the budget change?
06
Chapter 6 · Preview

Why does the budget change?

Population growth, inflation, new development, tourism, state legislation, emergencies, grants, and shifting community priorities all shape the budget over time. Budgets evolve because communities evolve.

Coming Next How can I explore the budget and have a voice?
07
Chapter 7 · Final Chapter · Preview

How can I explore the budget and have a voice?

Understanding the budget is only the beginning. Interactive exploration tools, public meetings, and open budget documents give residents real opportunities to participate — and to make informed decisions about the community they live in.
